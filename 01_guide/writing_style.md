# Writing style

For every document, brief, index, commit message and code comment in a project. The readers are
scientists, often reading in a second language, and later sessions that take every sentence
literally. Each sentence should be a fact, a reason, a request or a decision.

## Rules

1. **Name the actor and the mechanism.** "The filter removed 40 of 300 rows because their scores
   were tied", not "Rows were impacted by filtering".
2. **Give each number its unit, denominator and set.** "12 of 48 designs (25 %) at cutoff 0.8", and
   both values for a change ("from 10 to 5 µM", not "2-fold").
3. **One term per idea.** Define a project term once and do not vary it. A later session reads two
   words as two things.
4. **Say how sure you are, and why, once.** "Tentative: one replicate", not a stack of qualifiers.
5. **Start with the content.** No "This section explains", no "Here is an update".
6. **Make every "it", "this" and "they" point to one thing.** Repeat the noun when in doubt.
7. **Dates as YYYY-MM-DD.** Relative dates ("yesterday", "last session") lose their meaning once
   committed.
8. **Paths in backticks**, relative to the repository root or the citing document, so the citation
   check can follow them.

## Patterns to avoid

These make prose read as machine-written, and they hide what is being claimed.

- **Reveals and punchlines:** "The answer: X.", "Here's the thing:", a sentence ending on a dash
  and a twist. State the point.
- **Contrast for effect:** "It's not X, it's Y" where nobody holds X. Keep a contrast only between
  real alternatives.
- **Inverted emphasis:** "What this shows is", "Central to this is", "Worth noting is". Put the
  subject first.
- **Fragments and rhythm devices:** "No signal. No contrast. Just noise.", triplets chosen for
  cadence, repeated parallel clauses.
- **Telling the reader how to feel or read:** "This raises an important question", "the right
  reading", "importantly", "notably", "crucially", "clearly", "honest", "genuinely".
- **Summary closers:** "In short", "Overall", a last sentence that restates the paragraph.
- **Stock words:** robust, leverage, seamless, delve, landscape, underscore, pivotal, nuanced,
  harness, unlock, elucidate.
- **Coined labels and agency for things:** a rule does not "decide", data do not "tell a story".
  Describe the thing instead of naming it.

## Code and comments

Code can be as good as you can make it. Each script opens with a docstring: what it computes, which
decision governs it, what it reads and what it writes. Comments go where a good engineer would put
them. No banners, emoji, narration, or "generated by" labels.

## Commit messages

See `conventions.md`, Version control. One sentence that states what is now true.
